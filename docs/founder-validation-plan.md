# Founder Validation Plan

ThoroughLoop is currently a public MVP in early feedback stage. The next validation goal is to test whether early-stage founders recognize the pain of scattered operating context and whether the diagnosis, action, decision, and review loop maps to a real workflow.

## Goal Of Validation

Validate whether ThoroughLoop solves a real founder operating pain before expanding product scope.

The goal is not to prove traction. The goal is to learn whether founders understand the loop, recognize the pain, and can imagine using it when their notes, calls, follow-ups, and decisions get messy.

## Target Audience

- Early-stage founders before they have mature operating cadence.
- Solo founders managing sales, customer, hiring, and investor context themselves.
- Founder-led teams without dedicated RevOps, BizOps, or Chief of Staff support.
- Founder's Office, BizOps, or startup operators who turn messy updates into actions.

## Validation Hypothesis

Early-stage founders do not need another place to store notes. They need a lightweight way to turn scattered operating context into one diagnosis, one founder action, one decision, and a review prompt.

If the hypothesis is true, founders should quickly recognize the pain, understand the loop without technical explanation, and name at least one real workflow where they would try it.

## What To Test

- Whether the pain statement feels familiar.
- Whether the founder understands what to paste into the tool.
- Whether the diagnosis feels useful enough to prompt action.
- Whether the memo is clear enough to use in a real weekly review.
- Whether one action feels more useful than a long task list.
- Whether saving one decision for review feels natural.
- Which use case resonates most: sales, customer feedback, hiring, or investor updates.
- What would block trust, usage, or repeat review.

## Founder Segments To Contact

- Seed-stage B2B founders running founder-led sales.
- Pre-seed founders still managing product feedback manually.
- Solo founders handling hiring without a structured process.
- Founders preparing investor updates from scattered notes.
- Operators supporting founder-led teams before formal operating systems exist.

## Recommended Sample Size

Start with 5 to 10 founder or operator conversations.

This is enough for directional learning, not statistical validation. The goal is to identify repeated pain patterns, confusing language, and whether the loop maps to real operating behavior.

## Outreach Questions

- Where does your operating context usually get scattered?
- What kind of founder decisions are hardest to revisit a week later?
- When notes pile up, what do you do today to turn them into action?
- Which part of this loop feels most useful: diagnosis, memo, action, decision, or review?
- Which part feels unnecessary or unnatural?
- What would make you trust or distrust the diagnosis?
- Would you use this for sales, customer feedback, hiring, investor updates, or something else?
- What would need to change before you would try it with your own context?

## Demo Script

1. Start with the pain: founder context is scattered across notes, calls, updates, and follow-ups.
2. Explain the loop: paste messy context, get a diagnosis, review a memo, save one action, save one decision, and revisit it later.
3. Use one realistic messy input, preferably sales pipeline confusion.
4. Show the diagnosis and ask whether it identifies the right bottleneck.
5. Show the founder memo and ask whether it is clear enough to act on.
6. Show the action and decision, then ask whether this would help them close the loop next week.
7. Ask what felt useful, what felt wrong, and which workflow they would test first.

## Strong Signals

- The founder names a real recent situation where the loop would have helped.
- They understand the product without needing a technical explanation.
- They say the review step is useful, not just the memo.
- They can point to one workflow where they would try it this week.
- They challenge the wording because they are mapping it to a real operating problem.
- They ask for a sample input or want to paste their own messy context.

## Weak Signals

- They only say it is interesting but cannot name a use case.
- They treat it like another note-taking tool.
- They focus only on the memo and ignore the action or review loop.
- They need repeated explanation to understand the workflow.
- They say the output feels generic or not tied to a real decision.
- They would only use it after it becomes a full CRM, PM tool, or team workspace.

## How To Capture Anonymized Learnings

Use a simple note after each conversation:

```text
Segment:
Main pain:
Most resonant use case:
Least clear part:
Strongest quote or paraphrase, anonymized:
Would they try it with real context:
Follow-up needed:
```

Keep names, company names, private customer details, metrics, and sensitive operating context out of public docs. If a learning is added to the repo later, summarize it as an anonymized pattern.

## What Not To Claim Until Validated

- Do not claim founder validation has happened.
- Do not claim customers, users, revenue, retention, or traction.
- Do not claim the product saves a specific amount of time.
- Do not claim it improves decision quality without evidence.
- Do not claim it replaces a CRM, PM system, Notion workspace, operator, or Chief of Staff.
- Do not claim production readiness, enterprise readiness, compliance readiness, or live integrations.
- Do not claim AI capabilities beyond the current deterministic product behavior.
