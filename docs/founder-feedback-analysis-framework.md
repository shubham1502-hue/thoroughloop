# Founder Feedback Analysis Framework

Use this framework after multiple founder conversations. The goal is to understand whether ThoroughLoop maps to a real operating workflow, not to search for compliments.

## How To Identify Recurring Pain Patterns

Look for repeated language across conversations:

- Founders mention scattered notes, calls, CRM updates, customer feedback, hiring feedback, or investor drafts without prompting.
- Founders describe decisions that are hard to revisit later.
- Founders already have a workaround, such as a weekly note, spreadsheet, Notion page, Slack thread, or founder checklist.
- Founders describe the cost of missed follow-up, not just the annoyance of messy notes.
- Founders connect the pain to one of the example workflows: sales, customer feedback, hiring, or investor updates.

Do not count a pain as recurring after one enthusiastic conversation. Look for the same pattern across several founders or operators.

## How To Cluster Founder Feedback

Cluster feedback by the job the founder is trying to do:

| Cluster | What To Look For | Example Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales pipeline clarity | Stuck deals, unclear follow-up, pricing confusion, proposal drift | Founder wants one account decision this week |
| Customer feedback overload | Too many requests, unclear product priority, support noise | Founder wants one product theme to test |
| Hiring process drift | Candidate notes, unclear owner, role changes | Founder wants one hiring decision and criteria |
| Investor update preparation | Raw updates, risks, asks, metrics, board notes | Founder wants a cleaner operating narrative |
| Weekly operating review | Mixed notes, open decisions, unclear focus | Founder wants one reviewable decision |

Use an "other" bucket only when the feedback does not fit the existing clusters. Review that bucket after every 5 conversations.

## How To Avoid Confirmation Bias

- Ask where the workflow breaks, not only where it helps.
- Ask what the founder would use today instead.
- Track weak signals with the same care as strong signals.
- Record exact objections instead of translating them into your preferred interpretation.
- Do not treat politeness as demand.
- Do not count "this is interesting" as validation.
- Separate product feedback from positioning feedback.
- Review notes 24 hours later before deciding what they mean.

## What Counts As Validation

Validation can include repeated evidence such as:

- Founders independently name the scattered context pain.
- Founders identify a recent real situation where the loop would have helped.
- Founders understand the diagnosis, action, decision, and review loop without technical explanation.
- Founders ask to try it with their own messy context.
- Founders say the review step is useful, not just the memo.
- Multiple founders point to the same use case as urgent.

## What Does Not Count As Validation

Do not count these as validation:

- Compliments on the README, demo, or interface.
- A founder saying the idea is interesting.
- A request for broad features before the core workflow is understood.
- Feedback from someone outside the target audience unless it reveals a clear operating pattern.
- One founder asking for a feature that no one else needs.
- Your own belief that the use case is obvious.

## When To Change Positioning

Change positioning when:

- Multiple founders describe the same pain using different language than the README.
- One use case consistently resonates more than the others.
- Founders misunderstand the product as a note-taking tool, CRM, or PM system.
- The review loop is valuable but buried too late in the explanation.
- The local-first and MVP boundaries confuse readers before the core value is clear.

Keep positioning changes small. Update the words before changing the product.

## When To Change Product Scope

Change product scope only when:

- The same workflow failure appears across several conversations.
- The current MVP cannot test the most important assumption.
- A requested change supports the core loop: diagnosis, memo, action, decision, and review.
- The change can be tested without adding accounts, database persistence, payments, team workspace, or live integrations.

Do not expand scope because the product feels small. Small scope is useful while the workflow is still being validated.

## When To Ignore Feature Requests

Ignore or defer feature requests when:

- The request turns ThoroughLoop into a CRM, PM suite, dashboard, or team workspace before the core loop is validated.
- The request depends on auth, database persistence, payments, or live integrations.
- The request solves a different user's problem.
- The request is a nice-to-have after the founder already understands the loop.
- The request comes from one person and does not match a recurring pain pattern.

Track ignored requests in the validation log so they can be revisited later if they repeat.
